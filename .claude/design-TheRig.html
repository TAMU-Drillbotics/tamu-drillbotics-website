§ 03 Mechanical STRUCTURE · DRILLSTRING · HYDRAULICS MECH-DOM
Mechanical Engineering

Materials, design, physics.

The rig is a precision instrument scaled down — a 1.2-metre tower built to drill repeatable holes, instrument every reaction, and survive autonomous operation.

The frame is fabricated from 8020 aluminum extrusion, T-slot profiled for rapid iteration on sensor and bracket placement. FEA-driven structural analysis sets a target deflection under 0.5 mm at peak weight-on-bit, validated against load-cell readings during dry runs.

The drillstring couples a PDC bit through a length-matched shaft to a direct-drive BLDC, with a fluid jacket carrying drilling mud to clear cuttings. Hydraulic flow is sized for an optimal jet impact factor; weight-on-bit and rotary speed are co-controlled to keep the bit operating in its target torque envelope.

Safety is structural, not bolted on. A plexiglass enclosure contains debris under failure modes, mechanical interlocks gate spin-up to a closed-and-latched door, and a hardware E-stop cuts power upstream of every motor driver.

§ 04 Electronics HARDWARE · SENSORS · POWER EE-DOM
Electronics Stack

From signal to actuator.

Fourteen sensor channels feed a central electronics enclosure that converts physical reality into a 1 kHz stream of state estimates — and back into commanded torque.

The sensor stack spans pressure transducers on the mud line, strain gauges and torque sensors at the bit, hall-effect encoders for RPM, optical depth measurement at the feed, and a 6-axis IMU for vibration spectroscopy. Each is selected for sampling rate, noise floor, and bus compatibility — not just on a parts-shelf basis.

Motor drivers sit on an isolated 48-volt rail with independent fuses per channel. Logic-level signals are opto-isolated before crossing into the high-current domain; every driver carries its own current sense, fed back to the controller for closed-loop torque.

The central electronics enclosure houses the STM32H7 controller, CAN transceiver, sensor breakout, and power conditioning on a 4-layer custom carrier — designed in-house, fabricated externally, hand-assembled in the lab.

§ 05 Software FIRMWARE · CONTROL · INTEGRATION SW-DOM
Software Stack & Control Loop

Code that controls steel.

A real-time control loop closes around every revolution of the bit — embedded C on bare-metal STM32, deterministic to the microsecond.

The firmware layer is built on FreeRTOS with priority-pinned tasks for sensor acquisition, control compute, and CAN dispatch. PID controllers regulate three coupled axes: drill RPM, feed rate, and weight-on-bit — with cross-axis decoupling tuned during dry runs.

An ML interfacing layer pipes a downsampled sensor frame over high-speed serial to a co-processor running inference, then receives gain-trim and setpoint deltas back. The model never has direct authority — control authority always sits with the deterministic PID core, with the ML layer recommending, not commanding.

Telemetry is broadcast over CAN to a host laptop running a Python dashboard; logs are written to a removable SD card at 1 kHz for offline analysis and model training.

SEC · 05 / 09 — SW
§ 06 Data ML · INFERENCE · SENSOR FUSION DATA-DOM
Data & Machine Learning

Sensor fusion meets machine learning.

D-01 · Sensor Fusion

Unified state from messy reality.

A Kalman-style estimator combines pressure, torque, RPM, depth, and IMU streams into a single bit-state vector — denoised, time-aligned, and physically-constrained. The fused estimate, not raw sensors, is what the controller and ML layer see.

D-02 · Model Training

Offline, dataset-driven, reproducible.

Recorded drill runs form a versioned dataset; models are trained in PyTorch with deterministic seeds and checkpointed to a registry. Each release ships with a model card, hyperparameter manifest, and evaluation harness.

D-03 · Real-Time Inference

On-rig, low-latency, advisory.

Quantized models run on a dedicated CUDA-accelerated edge module beside the controller. The inference pipeline is hard-budgeted: drop a frame, miss a deadline, fall through to PID-only — never block the loop.
